Experimental design
Working outline · Overnight comparison · 20 October 2026
Research question: Under equal wall-time allowances, which agent interaction strategy achieves the highest completed validation ROC-AUC by the deadline? Track time to a 0.93 milestone and attainment of a 0.95 stretch goal, with token use and monetary cost alongside performance. AUC measures ranking performance, not the percentage of predictions classified correctly.
Core idea and hypothesis
NightAgent acts as a research apprentice: a researcher agent chooses and runs classification experiments in every condition. In the four supervised conditions, a supervisor reviews evidence and recommends subsequent work; the researcher-alone condition has no supervisor. We vary their interaction protocol. Structured supervision may improve experiment selection, but communication, explicit reflection, and supervisor literature research consume time that could otherwise be spent testing classifiers.
Example framing question: “Does the supervisor identify useful experiments, or spend the researcher’s time talking?”
“Optimal” means best among the tested strategies on the chosen task and budget. One dataset supports a pilot conclusion, not a universal claim about multi-agent systems.
Supervisor role: scientific strategy and Night Science
Use the Night Science principles from the brainstorm as a shared supervisor instruction in all four supervised conditions. The supervisor chooses a strategy in response to the evidence; these are feedback strategies within the role, not additional experimental conditions.
Explore before judging: consider alternative explanations and promising approaches before dismissing an idea after one disappointing result.
Reframe the question: turn a plateau into a testable question—for MAGIC, is performance limited by preprocessing or classifier capacity? Reframe the working hypothesis while keeping the dataset, evaluation, and search-space constraints fixed.
Connect across fields: suggest relevant analogies or methods from other scientific domains, treating transferred ideas as hypotheses to test. Only the fifth condition can seek external literature to support them.
Balance openness with structure: move from possible explanations to a concrete comparison that fits the remaining time and permitted tools.
Test ideas against evidence: reference completed runs, separate observations from speculation, and recommend controlled comparisons or replication when uncertainty warrants it.
Recognize practical limits: identify approaches that are too costly or uninformative and recommend redirecting effort within the remaining allowance; the harness still controls the run deadline.
At each scheduled review, give a concise assessment of the evidence, an unresolved question, and a recommended next action with its rationale and expected informative outcome. Log the strategy used and relevant run IDs. The researcher chooses and executes experiments; the supervisor guides and critiques them. This review is distinct from the separate researcher reflection step in conditions four and five.
Keep this supervisor instruction and feedback format fixed across the supervised conditions. The fifth condition adds literature evidence to the same role. The current comparison tests these supervision workflows; it does not isolate the effect of Night Science principles themselves.
Main interaction strategies
Researcher alone: the agent plans, runs experiments, and revises its approach without a supervisor or a separate explicit reflection step. It has no browser access.
Frequent supervision: the researcher receives supervisor feedback after each experiment. There is no separate explicit researcher reflection step, and neither agent has browser access.
Periodic supervision: the researcher receives supervisor feedback after each block of three experiments. There is no separate explicit researcher reflection step, and neither agent has browser access.
Periodic supervision plus reflection: supervisor feedback follows each block of three experiments, with a separate explicit researcher reflection step between experiments. Neither agent has browser access.
Periodic supervision plus reflection and supervisor literature review: the researcher reflects between experiments as in the fourth condition; the supervisor can browse literature during its review after each block of three experiments and use the findings in its feedback. Only the supervisor has browser access.
These five conditions form the main comparison. Ordinary planning and adaptation remain available to the researcher in every condition; “explicit reflection” means the additional scheduled step in the fourth and fifth conditions. Keep supervisor instructions and feedback format matched apart from the literature capability. Compare periodic supervision with and without explicit researcher reflection, then compare the reflection condition with and without supervisor literature review. Include a fixed classifier as a task-difficulty reference; automated random search is an optional extension, not a sixth main condition.
Supervisor literature review
Question: Does a supervisor who consults the literature recommend better experiments soon enough to justify the time spent reading?
Compare the fourth condition (periodic supervision plus reflection) with the fifth, which adds supervisor browser access during reviews after every three experiments. Keep review frequency, researcher reflection, and other resources matched. The researcher does not browse; it receives the supervisor’s evidence-backed recommendations, reflects, and chooses and runs the next experiment.
The supervisor identifies an uncertainty in the researcher’s results, searches papers or technical documentation, and incorporates relevant evidence into its feedback—or explains why no change is warranted. For MAGIC, it might investigate whether nonlinear relationships among shower-image features justify a different classifier or preprocessing choice within the shared search space.
Set a short per-supervisor-review browsing cap before launch. The supervisor’s searching, reading, and summarizing consume the same run allowance and count toward total cost. Retain the main outcome of best AUC by the deadline, with the same milestone and stretch goal.
Log the supervisor’s research question, queries, source URLs and supporting passages, recommendation, and the researcher’s response and resulting experiment ID; retain unhelpful findings too. The demo can show the path from source to supervisor feedback to measured result. A cited recommendation is evidence to test, not proof of improvement.
Shared task and controls
Possible workshop example: MAGIC Gamma Telescope
Use MAGIC as one possible application of this design: distinguish gamma-ray signal events from hadronic cosmic-ray background using 19,020 Monte Carlo–simulated telescope events and ten numerical features describing particle-shower images. This gives the classification task a physical detection context while keeping experiments inexpensive.
Example scientific brief: “Can a team of AI scientists distinguish gamma-ray signals from cosmic-ray background—and does supervision help them discover better classifiers faster?”
A small local pilot reached approximately 0.93–0.94 with tree-based models, so 0.95 is not yet demonstrated as achievable within the proposed search space. On the pilot machine, histogram gradient boosting took roughly 0.2–0.6 seconds and a 200-tree random forest about five seconds per fit and validation prediction. These are feasibility observations, not guarantees under the final validation protocol.
Dataset source: UCI MAGIC Gamma Telescope.
Start with one labeled binary-classification dataset and a common, bounded classifier search space. For a tabular task such as this example, proposed families are logistic regression, random forest, and histogram gradient boosting. Pilot their runtimes on the actual dataset and hardware before confirming the menu. Bound iterations, tree counts, and depth as applicable; enable training-only early stopping where supported. The researcher chooses preprocessing, configurations, and follow-up experiments through inspect_project, run_analysis, and read_results. The supervisor reviews the project and results and recommends actions; it does not execute classifier trials. In the fifth condition only, it also searches and reads external literature.
Hold constant the underlying language model and settings, shared task brief, classifier search space, data splits, hardware allocation, concurrency, and memory policy. Keep each role’s tools and instructions matched wherever that role is present, except for the specified review frequency, explicit researcher reflection, and supervisor browser access in the fifth condition. Reset memory between runs. Apply the same wall-time allowance, secondary spending ceiling, and experiment cap to each independent run, counting all agents together. Human intervention is limited to shared setup; the harness enforces limits.
Overnight schedule and resource limits
Study level: set a hard overnight completion deadline and reserve time for final test evaluation, aggregation, and reporting. Allocate the remaining search window across all five conditions and their repeats before launch.
Run level: each independent agent run receives the same elapsed-time allowance. Start its clock immediately before its first model call, after identical environment/data provisioning. Count researcher planning and reflection, supervisor feedback and literature research, all tool execution, waiting, retries, and failed attempts when applicable to that condition. Continue after reaching either the 0.93 milestone or the 0.95 stretch goal until the deadline; retain the current best result throughout.
Experiment level: define one trial as one classifier/pipeline configuration evaluated under the fixed validation protocol, including all required folds. Apply a hard timeout to its preprocessing, training, and scoring, bounded by the run’s remaining time. Timed-out and failed trials count toward the experiment cap and consume budget; incomplete results cannot update the best AUC.
Wall time is the main constraint. Dollar and trial-count ceilings are secondary safeguards, calibrated in the pilot to avoid routinely ending runs early. Record the actual stop time and reason when a safeguard or failure ends a run; carry forward its last valid result to the scheduled deadline and report early stops separately. Do not assume unfinished work would have succeeded.
Illustrative schedule, not a fixed commitment: five conditions × five repeats × fifteen minutes = 25 runs and six hours fifteen minutes of sequential search, leaving one hour forty-five minutes in an eight-hour window for overhead and evaluation. All five conditions receive the same run allowance and repeat count; supervisor browsing time is included in the literature condition’s fifteen minutes. Confirm the allowance, repeats, and reporting reserve after timing agent calls, browsing, and classifier trials. Parallel execution requires equal reserved resources per run and sufficient total capacity.
Evaluation and stopping
The researcher fits models and preprocessing on training data only; a fixed validation protocol supplies feedback for the researcher and, where present, the supervisor. Keep test data inaccessible to both agents during search. Use appropriate grouped or temporal splits if the dataset requires them.
The evaluation harness records the first completed, verified validation ROC-AUC ≥ 0.93 with its elapsed time, cumulative cost, and classifier artifact. It records the first crossing of 0.95 separately as stretch-goal attainment. Neither threshold ends the search. At the deadline, the harness freezes the classifier with the highest completed validation AUC, breaking ties by earliest completion. Cancel remaining work and exclude results completed after the deadline. Persist completed results and artifacts as the run progresses.
After search, the evaluation harness evaluates the saved first-milestone classifier (if any), first-stretch-goal classifier (if any), and deadline-selected classifier once each on the untouched test set; evaluate identical artifacts only once. Keep these checks outside the search loop and hidden from both agents. Report validation attainment and test confirmation separately. Pilot the example dataset under the exact validation protocol before the study. Any justified change to the milestone or stretch goal must be documented and frozen before the comparison.
Outcomes and cost accounting
Primary outcome: best completed validation ROC-AUC at the common run deadline. Compare this outcome across all runs, including those that never reach either threshold. Report the held-out test AUC of the deadline-selected classifier separately as a generalization check. Time to milestone, stretch-goal attainment, and cost provide supporting comparisons; they do not replace the main outcome.
Secondary outcomes: elapsed time to first validation ROC-AUC ≥ 0.93 and fraction of runs reaching that milestone by the deadline; fraction reaching the 0.95 stretch goal and time to first attainment when reached; dollars and tokens to each threshold and total dollars and tokens through stopping. Include all researcher/supervisor calls, planning, reflection, literature research, review, retries, and failed calls. Record input, output, and cached tokens with applicable prices. Track training/tool compute separately and state what the dollar total includes. Meter post-run evaluation/reporting separately.
Retain runs that never reach 0.93 as milestone-not-reached observations; their actual time-to-milestone is unknown, not equal to the time limit. Track non-attainment of the 0.95 stretch goal separately; it does not invalidate a run. Distinguish deadline completion, secondary-cap stops, and failures. Summarize attainment across all runs rather than averaging crossing times only among successful runs. If no valid classifier completes, report that explicitly.
Lead with best completed validation AUC at the deadline and best-so-far validation AUC versus elapsed time, marking the common deadline and the 0.93 and 0.95 reference levels. Add fraction reaching each threshold versus elapsed time, plus cost/token views to reveal trade-offs. Runs ending early retain their last completed result, and their stop reason remains visible. Tokens per dollar alone is not a measure of research efficiency.
Replication and demo
Repeat each condition with fresh agent runs and matched data splits and training seeds across strategies. Choose repeat count after a timing/cost pilot; randomize or interleave condition order to reduce time-of-night and service-load effects. Summarize variability and treat small samples as exploratory. Log trial start/end times, configurations, metrics, feedback, decisions, resource use, and stop reasons.
The demo shows each strategy’s experiments, feedback, progress through its deadline, and any crossings of the 0.93 milestone or 0.95 stretch goal. Lead with deadline performance, then compare milestone speed, cost, and reproducibility. Qualitative trace reviews help explain differences without replacing the measured outcomes.
